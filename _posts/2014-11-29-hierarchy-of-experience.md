![[hierarchy-of-experience-pyramids.jpeg|The traditional UX Hierarchy of Needs pyramid next to the reimagined version]]

I might be treading in territory I don’t belong with this one, but after being introduced to Technical Debt and the UX Hierarchy of Needs by [James Martin](http://www.linkedin.com/in/martinjamesf), I couldn’t stop thinking about the traditional graphic representation. I’m an engineer, so I look for function in everything, and although the underlying concepts were interesting, the stacked pyramid just wasn’t very useful.

![[hierarchy-of-experience-stacked.jpeg|The UX Hierarchy of Needs as a stacked pyramid: function, reliability, usability, proficiency, pleasurability]]

I have also been thinking a lot about lean startup method lately. Any design process or methodology should always include opportunities to test, and the hierarchy of needs didn’t seem any different. But the first question I wrestled with was do you really start with **Function** and then move onto **Reliability** and so on, or do you need a little bit of everything right from the start. So to solve my curiosity, I turned the graph on its side.

![[hierarchy-of-experience-sideways.jpeg|The hierarchy turned on its side, with every level present from the start]]

It immediately seemed more functional. Working from the bottom up, you can try to achieve the desired functionality, but still maintain sight of higher level goals. It also seemed to solve my desire to include tests along the way.

![[hierarchy-of-experience-tests.jpeg|The sideways hierarchy with test points marked along the way]]

![[hierarchy-of-experience-stage-1.jpeg|Stage one of the hierarchy over time|column=3]]![[hierarchy-of-experience-stage-2.jpeg|Stage two of the hierarchy over time]]![[hierarchy-of-experience-stage-3.jpeg|Stage three of the hierarchy over time]]

At any stage of development, there is always a component of each experience need. Then when you’ve reached your desired functionality you can test that, but also test if you are on target to reach that next goal. That stays true until you (hopefully) reach your ultimate goal.

Let me know what you think. I’m excited to experiment with it more. I’d love to hear your comments (in this post or on [twitter](https://twitter.com/jonalling)).
