The longer, two-fluted sibling of [[bit-8515a227|U02]] — same Kyocera line, same TiCN coating, same 30° helix, but two flutes instead of three and 1-1/8" of cut instead of 3/4". Those two changes matter more than they sound: two flutes means real chip gullets, and 1.125" means it can actually cut through 3/4" stock.

Aluminium chiploads run higher than on U02 despite similar feeds, because two flutes divide the feed two ways instead of three. Two flutes is also the better aluminium geometry — more room per chip, and aluminium makes long stringy ones.

## Notes

**This one tolerates wood, unlike U02.** Two flutes is the correct count for timber, and there is enough cutting length to go through 3/4" stock. But it is still a metal-cutting end mill: the gullets are tighter than a router spiral's and the rake is less aggressive, so it packs sooner and burns more readily. Reach for [[bit-81618|U01]] first and treat this as the spare when U01 is dull or already in the collet for something else. There is a hardwood preset for exactly that situation, marked *backup only*.

**Lubricate in aluminium.** Aluminium has an affinity for titanium-based coatings and will gall onto TiCN. WD-40 or denatured alcohol, misted. ZrN, DLC, or polished uncoated carbide would be the right coating for aluminium — this works in spite of its coating, not because of it.

**Steel is out of reach**, same as U02. A 1/4" carbide end mill in mild steel wants 4,000–5,300 rpm; the X-Carve Pro floors at 8,000. No coolant, no rigidity, no ferrous work.

**Diameter runs undersize by design** — up to 0.002" small. Noise in wood, but on a precision aluminium fit it leaves outside profiles up to 0.002" oversize. Measure a test cut and set a wear offset when the fit matters.

Fibreglass and G10 are where the TiCN genuinely earns its place — abrasive enough to strip uncoated carbide. Extraction and a respirator; the dust is a lung hazard.
