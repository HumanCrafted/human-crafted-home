The wide one. Three carbide-tipped straight flutes cutting a 1-1/8" swath from a 1/4" shank. For skimming the spoilboard flat and flattening panels and slabs too wide for the planer.

## Feeds & Speeds

For the X-Carve Pro and its 1.5 kW spindle. These match the presets stored on this tool in the Fusion hub library. Stepdown here is depth per facing pass; stepover is 0.90" (80% of diameter).

| Material | RPM | Feed | Chipload | Plunge | Stepdown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hard maple / oak | 12,000 | 280 ipm | 0.0078" | 20 ipm | 0.020" |
| Soft maple / walnut / cherry | 12,000 | 300 ipm | 0.0083" | 20 ipm | 0.030" |
| Plywood | 10,000 | 300 ipm | 0.0100" | 20 ipm | 0.030" |
| MDF / laminate | 10,000 | 300 ipm | 0.0100" | 25 ipm | 0.020" |
| Softwood / pine | 10,000 | 300 ipm | 0.0100" | 25 ipm | 0.040" |
| Acrylic / HDPE | 8,000 | 200 ipm | 0.0083" | 15 ipm | 0.020" |

The RPMs sit lower than the spiral bits because three flutes split the 300 ipm firmware cap three ways. At 16,000 rpm the cap would hold chipload to 0.006" and the tips would burnish more than cut. 12,000 and down is where a real chipload lives on this machine.

## Notes

**Depth per pass is a power budget, not a strength limit.** A 0.020" pass at full width and 280 ipm is already about 5 in³/min in hard maple, which is most of what the spindle has at 12,000 rpm. Take shallow passes and let the width do the work.

**Start every pass off the edge of the stock.** Fusion's Face operation leads in from outside the perimeter by default; keep it that way. This is not a plunge bit.

**Cutting height is 0.500"**, so that caps any rabbet or step cut with it. And at 1-13/16" overall it's the shortest bit in the drawer, which puts the collet nut close to the work. Fine for spoilboards and open panels; wrong for reaching into anything with walls.

On acrylic the melting risk is rim speed, not chipload. At this diameter even the 8,000 rpm floor is over 2,300 SFM, so keep the feed up and don't let it dwell.
