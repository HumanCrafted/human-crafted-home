The odd one out — a machinist's end mill rather than a router bit. Made by Kyocera, sold under McMaster's own part number. Three flutes, 30° helix, TiCN coated, centre cutting. It lives in the drawer for aluminium and fibreglass, and it is the wrong tool for every piece of wood in the shop.

## Notes

**Not for wood.** Three flutes means three small chip gullets, and wood chips are bulky and fibrous rather than the tight curls metal makes. They pack, heat builds, and the cut burns — which in maple leaves scorch you cannot sand out at depth. Router spirals are one or two flutes for exactly this reason. Use [[bit-81618|U01]] or [[bit-420-dnc250|D01]] instead.

**Steel is out of reach on this machine**, despite being what TiCN is really for. A 1/4" carbide end mill in mild steel wants roughly 250–350 SFM, or about 4,000–5,300 rpm. The X-Carve Pro's spindle floor is 8,000 rpm, which works out to 523 SFM — far past where carbide survives in steel. Add no coolant and a machine built for wood, and ferrous work simply isn't on the table.

**Lubricate in aluminium.** Aluminium has a chemical affinity for titanium-based coatings and will gall onto TiCN. A mist of WD-40 or denatured alcohol makes the difference between a clean wall and a welded edge. ZrN, DLC, or plain polished carbide would be the right coating here; this one works in aluminium in spite of its coating, not because of it.

**Diameter runs undersize by design.** The tolerance is −0.002" to 0.000", so the cutter may be as small as 0.248". On wood that is noise; on a precision aluminium fit it means parts come out up to 0.002" oversize on outside profiles. Measure a test cut and set a wear offset if the fit matters.

**Maximum cut depth 0.750".** Same limit as [[bit-46202-k|D02]] — it cannot cut through 3/4" stock.

Fibreglass and G10 are the one place the TiCN genuinely pays off, since both are abrasive enough to strip uncoated carbide. Run extraction and a respirator — the dust is an irritant and a lung hazard.
